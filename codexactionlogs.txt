## 2026-09-10 (Windows working copy) — headline row pinned so the gear no longer drags the RPM bars sideways

Action:
- Stopped the speedometer's RPM bars and DRS block from moving as the gear and speed change, by giving the two large numerals reserved boxes instead of letting their natural text width drive the flex row.
- Reported by the user as: "currently as the speed changes, the gear and rpm section gets shifted."

Files changed:
- `web/static/mock-driver.html` (only file changed)

Previous state:
- `.head .speed .val` had no reserved box, and `.gear .val` had none either. Both were plain inline numerals, so each was exactly as wide as the glyphs currently in it. `.head` is a flex row, so any change in a child's intrinsic width reflows every sibling after it.
- `.head .speed .lbl` was unaligned, so "SPEED" sat at the left edge of the speed block while the number was pushed right against `km/h`.

Root cause (measured, not assumed):
- The Formula1 display webfont has no `tnum` OpenType feature. `font-variant-numeric:tabular-nums` is therefore silently a no-op for it, and every digit has its own width. Measured directly on the rendered face: for the string `1111`, `plain 500.4px` and `tnum 500.4px` — identical, i.e. the feature does nothing. Browsers cannot synthesise tabular figures for a font that lacks them.
- Digit advance widths at the rendered 198px size: `0`=167.1, `1`=125.1, `2`=137.0, `3`=141.5, `4`=161.7, `5`=140.4, `6`=151.2, `7`=135.7, `8`=148.7, `9`=151.2, and `N`=192.2. That is a spread of up to 67px between `1` and `N`, so the gear box changed width by that much whenever the display switched between them, and pulled the RPM bars and DRS block along with it.
- An earlier attempt at this had added `tabular-nums` to both numerals and pinned the speed with `min-width:3ch`. That appeared to work only by accident: `ch` is the width of the `0` glyph, which is the widest digit, so `3ch` happens to equal the widest possible three-digit string. The gear had no such box and kept shifting — measured at `span_bars_x: 28.91px` over the run below.

New state:
- `.head .speed .val` keeps `display:inline-block;min-width:3ch;text-align:right` — now documented as load-bearing rather than incidental.
- `.gear .val` gained `display:inline-block;min-width:1.2ch;text-align:center`, so the widest gear glyph (`N` at 192.2px) fits without the box ever resizing. `min-width` rather than `width` so an unexpectedly wider glyph grows the box instead of being clipped.
- `.head .speed .lbl{text-align:right}` so the "SPEED" label sits over the number rather than stranded at the far left of the block.
- Both rules carry a comment explaining that the Formula1 face has no `tnum` feature and that `ch` is the width of the widest digit, so the next person does not "clean up" the reserved boxes as redundant.
- `font-variant-numeric:tabular-nums` was left on both numerals. It is a no-op for this font, costs nothing, and keeps the intent legible if the display font is ever swapped for one that has the feature.

Why the previous fix was not enough. The earlier change gave the sector labels a
nine-candidate search: the midpoint plus eight directions at a reach of k*0.085 (123
units). Two things made that insufficient, both measured before anything was edited.

1. The reach was shorter than the problem. The label box is 143x89 units and a turn number
   is 43 tall, so a label needs to clear the centreline by (89+43)/2 = 66 units to be
   reliably off both the tarmac and the numbers. Measured, the old search's chosen spots
   cleared by 34, 21 and 59 units - still on the racing line. Legal spots exist but the
   nearest ones start 135-200 units out, past the 123-unit cap.
2. The midpoint is an arbitrary place to stand. A sector is 1667 units of road and the
   label sat opposite whatever the halfway point happened to be. Stepping straight out
   along the road normal from S2's midpoint needs 350 units to find air, because the lap
   doubles back there; S1's own normal never becomes legal at all.

The panel was also too tight, so `pad` went from k*0.06 to k*0.10 - a 144x89 label cannot
sit outside a shape whose margin is 87 units.

Previous state (sector label block in setTrack):
    const sfs = k * 0.062, sreach = k * 0.085;

New state:
- `lapPts` - the lap sampled once per setTrack into 270 points (one every 19 units against
  a 35-unit-wide road). `boxRoadGap(box, n)` now reads this array at a stride instead of
  calling `circuit.getPointAtLength` per sample. Not a micro-optimisation: the new search
  evaluates a few thousand candidate boxes per label and `getPointAtLength` is a real
  geometry query, so leaving it in would have meant roughly a million calls per page load.
  The stride is chosen so n=90 lands on the same positions the START search used before,
  leaving that label's chosen anchor unchanged.
- The sector search now SLIDES the label along its own sector: 8 anchors spanning 15%-85%
  of the sector, the road normal at each (via the existing `tangentAt`), a +/-40 degree fan
  around that normal in 20-degree steps, both sides of the road, and a distance sweep from
  k*0.04 to k*0.28 in k*0.010 steps.
- Scoring: min of (road clearance, gap to each of the 23 turn boxes, gap to each
  already-placed sector label). The NEAREST candidate clearing everything by k*0.050 wins,
  ties broken on clearance. Nearest rather than roomiest, because a sector label that has
  wandered across the map stops naming anything.
- Candidates must lie inside the padded panel (bbox +/- pad, inset k*0.012) rather than the
  raw bbox as before - past that edge the label is outside the viewBox and clipped.
- The fallback is now the roomiest candidate found rather than the midpoint, so a shape
